Estimated tax at extension

The February deadline means projecting before every document arrives, so the estimate runs twice: once in February to file, and again in early April to true up. A 4868 extends the time to file, not the time to pay, so the payment number has to be right by April 15 whenever the form itself was filed.

Tiers (set in projection week 1)

TierWhoMethodReview time
1 Refund likelyW-2 withholding plus estimates comfortably cover projected taxAutomated projection; extend with $0 payment5 min
2 Balance due, predictableBrokerage, rentals, retirement income, modest K-1sProjection in Axcess using the 2025 return rolled forward, plus 2026 docs on hand15 min
3 VolatileLarge K-1s, equity comp, big gains, multi-stateTier 2 method plus K-1 estimates requested from issuers in January, and a cushion (e.g., 10%)30 min

At a 40/40/20 mix of 170 extended returns, reviewer time is about 6 + 17 + 17 = 40 hours across two weeks.

What the projection computes

  • 2026 balance due: projected federal and state tax, minus withholding, 2026 estimates and any 2025 overpayment applied.
  • Penalty exposure (Form 2210): compare payments made to the prior-year safe harbor, which is 100% of 2025 tax, or 110% if 2025 AGI was over $150,000 ($75,000 MFS). This is why the 2025 base year has to be in the system before January (see pre-work).
  • Payment: the extension payment for 4/15, plus a draft 2027 Q1 estimate due the same day.
  • States: some states accept the federal extension automatically but still want a payment voucher when tax is owed; others need their own extension form. Keep a state matrix in Karbon so the batch knows which forms each client needs.

Mechanics of an early filing

  • Filing the 4868 on 2/26 with an electronic funds withdrawal needs Form 8878 signed. Send it in the same eSign envelope as the extension letter.
  • The IRS lets you schedule the debit for a later date up to the due date, so a 2/26 filing can still debit on 4/15. confirm in your Axcess release Otherwise file with no payment and have clients pay by 4/15 through Direct Pay or EFTPS, choosing "extension" as the payment type.
  • The April true-up only changes the payment, not the extension, so top-ups go through Direct Pay or EFTPS.

Can the stack run overnight?

Yes for orchestration, ingestion and drafting. No for transmitting. Windmill is a job scheduler with cron triggers, so it can run every step that has an API or webhook. Karbon has a documented REST API (work items, notes, file upload). StanfordTax processes documents as they arrive and syncs with Karbon. Filed and Axcess are cloud services, so no desk machine has to stay on. Everything that files or signs stays a daytime, human step.

Rules for overnight jobs

  • Jobs only touch returns in the "Ready for prep" Karbon status. A return a person has open in Axcess is skipped.
  • Nothing transmits overnight. E-file, 8879/8878 eSign and extension batches run after reviewer sign-off.
  • Every job is safe to re-run (it checks what it already did) and writes a log line to the Karbon work item.
  • Run a self-hosted Windmill or a tenant whose data agreement covers taxpayer data, and add it to your WISP (FTC Safeguards Rule).

Memo template (two parts in one PDF)

  1. Preparation facts, page 1, a fixed table Filed can read reliably: filing status changes, dependents (name, relationship, months lived with client), new or closed income sources, estimated payments (date and amount), expected K-1s (issuer, expected date), home or vehicle purchases and sales, retirement distributions and rollovers, state moves and days worked out of state, items needing an organizer follow-up.
  2. Narrative memo on the firm template: attendees and date, life events, planning topics raised, judgment calls the preparer needs to make, open items with owner and due date, and quotes for anything the client asserted that isn't backed by a document.

Put a banner at the top: "Client representations from interview. Not verified against source documents." Filed and the preparer should treat interview facts as leads that need support, not as data to enter.

How Filed takes it in

Filed's documented pattern is to read source documents and enter data into the tax software. The memo reaches it like any other PDF, as long as it sits wherever Filed pulls documents from (the StanfordTax binder or the Karbon work item). confirm which source Filed reads, and whether it accepts firm instructions per return If Filed accepts per-return notes, also send the Preparation facts as structured text. That's more reliable than having Filed read it out of a PDF.

Before the first recording: several states require every party to consent to a recording. Put recording consent in the engagement letter and confirm it out loud at the start of each call. Get §7216 consent covering disclosure of return information to the AI vendors in the chain. Check each vendor's data-retention terms, especially read.ai's.

Pre-work: October to December 2026

Bring 2025 returns in as the base year

Use three layers, from most to least authoritative:

  1. Axcess proforma rollover (native): rolls 2025 into the 2026 return, including carryovers, depreciation and prior-year comparison columns. Run it when the TY2026 Axcess release ships, usually in December.
  2. StanfordTax organizers (native): StanfordTax reads prior-year data from Axcess to pre-fill each organizer with 2025 numbers and a document checklist built from 2025 payers. A 1099 issuer from 2025 with no 2026 match becomes an automatic "missing?" flag.
  3. Firm base-year table (Windmill's Postgres): one row per client, which the projections, safe-harbor math and law-change screens all query. Axcess doesn't make this kind of cross-client query easy.

Filling the base-year table

  • Source: ask Wolters Kluwer what Axcess Tax data export your license includes (an API or a bulk export). verify As a fallback, have a Windmill job read each final 2025 client copy (PDF) with an LLM extractor.
  • Fields: filing status, state(s), wages, interest, dividends, capital gain, IRA/pension, Social Security, Sch C/E/K-1 totals, AGI, deduction type and amount, taxable income, total tax, withholding, estimates, refund or balance due, applied overpayment, and each carryover (capital loss, passive, AMT credit, foreign tax credit, charitable, NOL, Form 8606 basis).
  • Automatic tie-out: AGI, total tax, payments and refund/balance due must foot to the client copy. Any row that doesn't goes to a person.
  • Run it the week after the October 15 deadline, once every 2025 return is final, and again after any amended return.

How the table gets used

  • 2026 safe-harbor amount for every client (100% or 110% of 2025 tax), checked against 2026 estimates paid, with year-end letters going out in December.
  • Starting point for every February projection.
  • Variance review: Filed's 2026 draft is compared line by line, and anything that moved more than 10% and $1,000 gets flagged for the reviewer.
  • Law-change screens: tag clients affected by provisions that start in 2026 (OBBBA): the 90% cap on gambling losses, the non-itemizer charitable deduction and 0.5%-of-AGI floor, the itemized-deduction value cap for the 37% bracket, the $7,500 dependent-care FSA limit, cost-basis reporting on 1099-DA, and the $2,000 threshold for 1099-NEC/MISC.

Axcess iQ + AnswerConnect alongside Filed?

They do three different jobs. Filed points out issues inside a return as part of preparation. AnswerConnect is a research library of primary sources and CCH explanations, which gives you something to cite. Axcess iQ searches your Axcess return data for clients affected by tax-law changes and drafts letters to them.

AnswerConnect: worth it if you don't already have a research library (Checkpoint, Bloomberg Tax, etc.) and it can be added to your Axcess contract at a reasonable price. Filed's suggestions aren't authority. When a reviewer questions a position, or an IRS notice does, you need a citation. With 250 individual returns you'll research maybe a few dozen issues a year, so compare the quote against a lighter research subscription. Wolters Kluwer doesn't publish prices.

Axcess iQ: skip it. It's a scan of the firm's data for law-change impacts, and your base-year table plus a handful of Windmill queries does the same for a 250-client individual practice, at no extra cost and with logic you control. Most of what I could find about iQ is vendor material from 2018–2022, so confirm it's still actively developed before you price it.

Confirm with vendors

  • Filed: does it have a trigger API or webhook for queueing returns; which document sources it reads (StanfordTax binder, Karbon, upload); whether it takes per-return firm instructions; whether it can draft extension projections.
  • StanfordTax: do files attached to a Karbon work item land in the binder automatically, or does the memo have to be uploaded to StanfordTax; is there an upload API.
  • Wolters Kluwer: what Axcess Tax data export or API your license includes; how batch e-filing of 4868s and state extensions works; future-dated debits on an early-filed 4868.
  • read.ai: API or webhook access on your plan, whether the full transcript is included, and retention and training-use terms.
  • Karbon: API rate limits for the nightly jobs.
